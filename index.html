Solana basics

How Solana Swap works

Trading SOL and SPL tokens from your wallet

Solana Swap is a non-custodial interface for exchanging SOL, USDC, USDT and other SPL tokens on Solana. You connect a wallet such as Phantom or Solflare, choose the two assets and review a quote before you sign.

SOL and SPL tokens

SOL is the native coin of Solana and pays for transaction fees. Every other asset is an SPL token, the network's token standard. Each SPL token you hold sits in its own token account, and routers use a wrapped form of SOL when a pool needs it, which the interface handles for you.

SOL vs SPL

Swapping SOL or an SPL token

Solana has no separate approval step, but a new token needs a place to land in your wallet.

In practice

If you receive a token for the first time, a token account is created for it in the same transaction. That takes a small refundable rent deposit in SOL, shown in the wallet prompt.

How to swap

How to swap on Solana

Route and quote

The interface searches Solana DEX pools, can split a trade across several of them, and shows the expected output, price impact and slippage tolerance. You confirm the quote before the wallet is asked to sign.

Step by step

  1. Connect your Solana wallet, such as Phantom or Solflare.
  2. Choose the sell token and the buy token, then enter an amount.
  3. Review the quote, route and network fee.
  4. Sign the swap in your wallet; it is sent to the network and confirms on-chain.
  5. Check your wallet for the bought token once the transaction is final.

If you wait too long to sign, the quote can go stale, and a refreshed one will be shown.

Fees & gas

What a Solana swap costs

Network fees

Fees on Solana are paid in SOL and are usually small compared with many other chains. Some wallets add an optional priority fee when the network is busy. See the Solana docs for how fees work.

Trading cost

A swap also carries price impact, your slippage tolerance and any pool or aggregator fee, all reflected in the quoted output. A failed transaction can still cost the network fee.

Keep SOL in the wallet

Holding USDC or USDT does not pay for fees. Keep a little SOL for the swap itself and for any token account deposits, or the wallet will not let you sign.

Security

Staying safe

Non-custodial by design

You connect your own wallet and sign every transaction yourself. A swap interface routes and quotes; it never holds your funds or keys.

Before you sign

  • Verify the token mint address; fake tokens with copied names and logos are common on Solana.
  • Read what your wallet says the transaction will do, including any token accounts it creates.
  • Check the quote, price impact and slippage before confirming.
  • Never share your seed phrase; a swap does not need it.
  • Smart contracts can hold bugs, so risk is never zero.
FAQ

Solana Swap FAQ

What can I do with Solana Swap?

You can swap SOL, USDC, USDT and other SPL tokens on Solana from your own wallet. The interface routes across Solana DEX pools, shows a quote, and you sign the transaction yourself.

Which wallets work with Solana swaps?

Any Solana wallet that can connect to a web app, such as Phantom or Solflare. The wallet holds your keys and asks you to approve each transaction.

Do I need SOL to swap USDC or USDT?

Yes. Transaction fees on Solana are paid in SOL, even when the token you are selling is a stablecoin. Keep a small SOL balance so you can sign the swap.

Why did my wallet ask for a small SOL deposit?

A token you have not held before needs its own token account, and creating it takes a small rent deposit in SOL. The deposit can be recovered if you close the account later.

Why would a Solana swap fail?

The price may have moved past your slippage tolerance, the quote may have gone stale before you signed, or the network may have been congested. A failed transaction can still cost the network fee, so review the quote and retry with a fresh one.

Does the site hold my tokens during a swap?

No. The swap runs from your wallet through on-chain programs, and the interface never holds your funds or private keys. You can reject any request in your wallet.
